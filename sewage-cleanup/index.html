Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Lacey Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Lacey, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Lacey

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems

A blocked sewer line on the property is the most common starting point. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Skip the mop and bucket, scrub any exposed skin and wait for a trained crew.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
