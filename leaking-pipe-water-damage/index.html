Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Lacey

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Lacey homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. That is our part of the job. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Keep Power AwayWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Coverage

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Soaked Wall Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. Left in a sealed cavity it can stay wet for a long time, which is why we usually take it out.

Swollen Cabinets

Under a sink, a cabinet base made of particleboard puffs up and falls apart after it takes on water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Mold Inside the Wall

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Damaged Flooring

Moisture from a leaking slab or wall line spreads out below the finished floor. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.
