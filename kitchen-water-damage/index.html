Kitchen Water Damage in Lacey

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Lacey homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

What We Do

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Under the Base Cabinets

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

The Ice Maker Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.
